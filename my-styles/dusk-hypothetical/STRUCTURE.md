# Dusk Hypothetical — Film structure

The act structure every film in this style follows (chosen by the channel on 2026-11-13). It is measured from the full transcripts and shot lists of the ten reference films (evidence: `analysis/sound-script.md` §1–4, `analysis/motion-depth.md` §3–5 of the reference set). Beat names are abstract forms: write your own premise, story and every line; never reuse a sentence, a character or a story from the reference.

Times are for the standard length of **≈ 10:30 (630 s)**; scale them by percentage for other lengths. Shorter films: see "Compressed" at the end.

## Acts

| Act | Share | Time (630 s) | What happens | Picture and pace |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 Hook | 8 % | 0:00–0:50 | **HOOK**: a live demo of the power (or the crisis) in an everyday public place, with a concrete number and often a small cash bet with friends → **REACT**: a bystander's amazed one-line question → **RULES** of the power → **PROMISE**: a three-item list of bigger payoffs ending on a foreshadow of the climax → **NOW-TURN** back to the small present, often with a comic slip | fastest minute: 25–36 shots, median ≈ 1.8 s; first shot an ECU of the hero's smug or deadpan face; title/question card at 0:30–0:55 |
| A2 Explainer | 8 % | 0:50–1:43 | **NORMAL**: what an ordinary person can do, with the first real number; the remaining rules, the limit and the cost law of the power | slows to 11–16 shots/min; longer graphic inserts (bars, comparisons) |
| B1 Personal gain | 19 % | 1:43–3:40 | 5–7 short vignettes (≈ 20 s each): bets won, school, sport, games, a date; one named science effect; **PLANT** one detail the climax will need (a fact, a faint side character, a kept-empty resource, a learned technique) | 18–24 shots/min; home set intercut with one-shot vignettes |
| B2 Public scale | 29 % | 3:40–6:43 | **LEVEL** marker (a short sentence of ≤ 6 words) → a bigger stage → **CRAZY** pivot ("the strangest part") → **WORLD** reacts: views, media, scientists, institutions, governments; first moral wobble | crowds of hundreds to thousands, huge wides, jumbotrons, phone screens, counters climbing |
| C1 Cost | 9 % | 6:43–7:40 | **COST**: one short standalone pivot sentence, then 4–6 quick consequences: the friend drifts (a bubble line), a family moment, identity or meaning lost; one fact | quieter places (bedroom, empty corridor), more close-ups |
| C2 Crisis | 16 % | 7:40–9:20 | **SKIP** marker (a year later, or a day count) → a life-or-death event that pays off the PLANT and an earlier fact → **COUNTDOWN** spoken → **ONLY-YOU** → the choice that gives up the gain the film was built on | burst of 27–34 shots/min around minute 8–9; mood flood (red alarm, blue night) |
| C3 Resolution | 6 % | 9:20–10:00 | success → a professional's verdict line → the friend reconciles → a callback to the opening place, gag or bet (7 of 10) | slower, warmer light, back to the opening place |
| D Sting | 4 % | 10:00–10:22 | a stranger, message or object offers a bigger version of the power (or the power passes to someone else) | one place, a held reveal |
| E Closing card | 1.5 % | 10:22–10:30 | yes/no question restating the premise, a comment prompt, like/subscribe tied to the climax choice | the closing choice card (§8) with the episode's cast in a row; held 5–10 s |

Turning points by share of runtime (median of ten): NOW-TURN 8 %, first gain 16 %, first LEVEL 35 % (19–43), WORLD 46 %, COST 64 % (46–75), SKIP 73 %, resolution 89 %, sting 95.5 %.

## Rhythm rules

- **Escalation:** 12–16 vignettes through B1–B2, one every 20–25 s; the power goes up a level 3–4 times (small → bigger → public → world, plus the skip into the climax).
- **Chapters:** a turn every 1:20–1:45, each opened by a short marker sentence (≤ 6 words): level-up, strangest part, cost, time skip.
- **Facts:** ≈ 1.1 real numbers or studies per minute (≈ 11–12 per 10 minutes), each a 1–3 sentence aside right after the action it explains; only A2 is an explainer block. Numbers as round values, "one in N", "N out of 10", then made tangible by a comparison. Every fact goes into SOURCES.md.
- **Open loops:** the PROMISE foreshadow (or a premise question), one to three plants, a teaser about every 90 s, and the sting that opens a new loop.
- **Side characters:** 5–10 % of sentences, 1–6 words each, always as speech bubbles; they cluster at five points: REACT (0:15–0:45), a bet or rival (B1), the friend's doubt (C1), a plea in the crisis (C2), the verdict and reconciliation (C3). Recurring types: a best friend, a rival, an authority, a child.
- **Running counter:** one number (money, followers, score, heart rate, seconds) restated at each beat and shown as a pill.
- **Sentences:** median 6 words (1–3 words 23 %, 4–6 words 37 %, 7–10 words 27 %, 16+ only 3 %); second person present; past tense only for facts and backstory; fragments (a number, a place, a sound) as beat stamps and countdowns; 0.4–1.1 questions per minute.
- **Voice:** first word at 0.0 s, 33–48 words in the first 15 s, no gap over ≈ 1.3 s anywhere, voice ends 0.3 s before the film.

## Places and shots per act

- Plan ≈ 30 places for 10:30: 1–2 home sets (school, street, bedroom) that the film keeps returning to (≈ 60 % of place changes are returns), and many one-shot places for vignettes and world reactions (stadium, studio, globe, shop, clinic).
- Every act opens a new place with a wide, huge or medium shot; inserts bridge to new places.
- Graphic inserts are densest in A2 and in the WORLD block; the closing card is the last shot.

## Variants

- **Explainer-led** (3 of 10): demo → NORMAL facts → a premise question and a mechanics explainer at 0:47–0:54, then the gains.
- **Compressed** (one 6-minute film): hook 0:00–0:47, explainer to 1:28, one gains block, COST at ≈ 46 %, SKIP at ≈ 58 %, resolution at ≈ 79 %, sting and closing card. Use these shares for films of 4–7 minutes.
- **Double length** (one 20-minute film): the same skeleton with two extra middle acts (backlash, a cure or escape attempt ladder) and a slower cut (≈ 14 shots/min).
- Rare: a skill instead of a power with an early climax; a tragic ending with a moral line and no closing card. Not the default.

## Titles and thumbnails

Title pattern: a second-person conditional question naming one power or condition, ≤ 8 words after the opener, the conditional word in capitals. The premise is one concrete, social, everyday power pushed to a multiplier, a totality or an extreme. Thumbnail and channel rules live in the channel profile.
